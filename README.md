# IT Career Atlas

## 🌐 Live Website

**[Open IT Career Atlas](https://it-career-atlas.netlify.app/)**

IT Career Atlas is an interactive knowledge guide for people exploring technology careers. It is designed to explain **career domains, the roles inside them, required capabilities, technologies, workflows, AI involvement, learning paths and job readiness** in a structured way.

## 🎯 Core Experience

The Atlas uses two directions of exploration:

**Horizontal Swipe → Explore major IT career domains**  
**Vertical Scroll ↓ → Explore the depth of the current domain**

```text
🏠 Home
   │

   ├── 02 Software Development
   ├── 03 Data
   ├── 04 AI & Machine Learning

   ├── 06 DevOps & SRE

   ├── 11 Business & Systems Analysis
```

> **Important:** These top-level pages are treated as **career domains**, not as universal job titles. A domain can contain multiple occupations, specialties and job titles, and real companies may combine responsibilities differently.

## 📚 Standard Content Model

Every domain is built around the same professional knowledge structure, while the actual content is adapted to that domain.

1. **Definition** — What the domain is and why it exists.
2. **Career Tree** — Roles and specializations within the domain.
3. **Core Skills** — Knowledge and capabilities needed for the work.
4. **Technology Stack** — Languages, platforms, frameworks and tools commonly used.
5. **AI · Manual · Hybrid** — Where AI can assist, where human work remains essential, and where both are combined.
6. **Work Process** — A realistic high-level workflow from problem to outcome.
7. **Learning Roadmap** — A practical progression from foundations to job readiness.
8. **Role Progression** — Examples of increasing responsibility, without implying one universal career ladder.
9. **Job-Readiness Checklist** — Practical evidence a learner can build through projects, skills and communication.

The site intentionally avoids making every section look like **number → giant heading → paragraph → empty space**. Information is presented through career trees, cards, workflows, roadmaps and checklists where those formats improve understanding.

## 🤖 AI Information Standard

AI is presented as part of the modern workflow, not as a replacement for every human responsibility.

The Atlas distinguishes between:

| Mode | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🤖 AI-assisted | AI can perform or accelerate parts of a task |
| 👤 Human-led | Human judgment, execution or accountability is central |
| 🤝 Hybrid | Human and AI work together with review and validation |

AI capabilities and tooling change quickly, so AI-related sections will be reviewed and updated rather than treated as permanent facts.

## 🧭 Information Quality Standard

Because this project is intended to help people make education and career decisions, information quality is treated as a core product requirement.

- Use authoritative occupational and workforce references where available.
- Distinguish **job titles, occupations, work roles, career domains and specializations** instead of treating them as identical.
- Avoid claiming that one degree, certificate, programming language or tool is universally required unless a reliable source supports that claim.
- Separate **common practice** from **mandatory requirements**.
- Avoid promising employment, salary or career outcomes.
- Keep technology lists representative rather than pretending there is one universal stack.
- Review fast-changing AI, cloud and security information periodically.
- Prefer practical explanations over unexplained jargon.

For cybersecurity terminology, the project uses the NIST NICE Framework as an important reference because it explicitly distinguishes work roles from jobs and occupations and organizes work using tasks, knowledge and skills.

## 📖 Reference Foundations

The content model is informed by established workforce and occupational references, including:

- NIST NICE Workforce Framework for Cybersecurity
- U.S. O*NET occupational information
- U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational information
- Official documentation from relevant technology platforms when specific technologies are discussed

These sources are references for accuracy, not a claim that the U.S. occupational structure maps perfectly to every country or employer. Local job markets and company requirements can differ.
